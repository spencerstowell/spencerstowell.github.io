My role / Method

Designing for lower actuator load.

A parallel elastic actuator (PEA) places a spring in parallel with a motor. The right spring reduces power and load requirements, extends runtime, and enables cheaper actuators for powered prostheses. As first author in BYU’s Compliant Mechanisms and Robotics Lab, I developed a regression-based method for choosing that spring.

  • Method. Optimal PEA stiffness is usually computed with convex optimization. I framed RMS-torque minimization as weighted least-squares regression (WLSR) on the task’s torque–deflection profile, weighting each point by its duration. The result is a single optimal spring with a simple formulation and visual intuition.
  • Richer springs. The same formulation fits piecewise-linear and polynomial stiffness models, enabling higher-complexity elastic elements such as piecewise springs.
  • Validation on external data. On ankle walking data from Collins et al., WLSR gave 219 Nm/rad, within the 180–240 Nm/rad range reported as metabolically optimal. Subject-specific fits ranged from 187 to 267 Nm/rad.
  • Benchtop validation. With collaborators at the University of Notre Dame, a motor-driven walking profile with a regression-optimized piecewise spring measured a 30% RMS-torque reduction (46% predicted from the task profile; 14% predicted from the experimental data).
