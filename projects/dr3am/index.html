Project description

What DR3AM was built to do.

DR3AM is a 4U CubeSat-format printer that extrudes photocuring resin and cures it with 395 nm UV light as it deposits. On a three-person payload team, I designed nearly all of its mechanical and mechatronic systems and presented the work at SmallSat 2025.

  • Low-power extrusion. Direct-ink-write deposition draws 3 W versus about 30 W for a heated FDM extruder, bringing total system power to 30 W versus a typical 70 W. A 20 mL syringe with an embedded lead screw, driven by a stepper and 19:1 planetary gearbox, extrudes at 30–40 steps/mm³, comparable to a standard printer.
  • Cylindrical motion. Selected an R–Z–θ architecture over Cartesian and delta options for space efficiency and robustness: a NEMA 8 radial gantry, synchronized dual NEMA 8 Z axes, and a NEMA 17 pancake stepper for continuous plate rotation. The result is a 700 cm³ print volume, 17.6% of the payload volume.
  • Custom Klipper and Mainsail. Adapted Klipper for direct-ink writing on a Raspberry Pi 4B, with a Mainsail browser interface for uploading files and running prints over Wi-Fi. Configured switchless homing on the radial and dual-Z axes.
  • Power and electronics. Specified two 6.4 V, 6 Ah LiFePO4 batteries in series (12.8 V), eight UV LEDs in 4S2P, an RPi Pico for stepper control, an Arduino Nano for safety logic, and an accelerometer for launch detection.
  • Flight safety. Designed laser-safety acrylic shielding, a fail-closed 12 V resin solenoid valve, and pull-pin measures for integration into an L3 rocket.

Engineering iteration

Flight was not the finish line.

DR3AM flew to 10,000 ft on an L3 rocket and printed before and after flight. It returned with broken zip ties, a bent radial gantry, and misaligned Z axes, which permanently reduced gantry accuracy; when the damage occurred (motor burn, chute deployment, or landing) is not known. The lessons for the next version: strengthen the radial gantry, reduce UV overexposure, and improve extrusion consistency.
